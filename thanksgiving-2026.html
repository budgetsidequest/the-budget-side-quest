The $25 Thanksgiving Side Quest

Thanksgiving dinner for ten people, a promotional code, and one slightly ridiculous grocery bargain.

Quest: Thanksgiving Grocery Run

Merchant: Walmart

Advertised price: About $35

Promo code used: DOUBLE10

Discount: $10

Gold spent: Approximately $25

Status: Supplies Acquired

Thanksgiving is coming, and apparently feeding ten people is supposed to cost $35 this year.

At least, that's what Walmart was advertising.

Now, $35 for an entire Thanksgiving dinner sounded pretty damn reasonable. But you know what's better than $35?

$25.

I found a $10 promotional code, DOUBLE10, and applied it to my qualifying Walmart order. Suddenly the Thanksgiving grocery run became a little more interesting.

If you're planning your own grocery run, the code might be worth trying. Promotional codes can expire or have restrictions, so there's no guarantee it'll still work.

⚔ The Loot

For roughly $25, I walked away with quite a haul:

  • A 12-pound Butterball turkey
  • Two boxes of Stove Top stuffing
  • Two packets of brown gravy
  • Green beans, corn, and fried onions
  • Jellied cranberry sauce
  • Sweet potatoes and russet potatoes
  • Cream of mushroom soup
  • Evaporated milk
  • Pumpkin filling and pie crust
  • Whipped cream and marshmallows

That's a pretty respectable Thanksgiving inventory.

Not everything went exactly according to the advertised bundle, of course.

I made a few selections based on what my family would actually eat. Jellied cranberry sauce, for example, beats pretending we're going to do something fancy with fresh cranberries.

The dinner rolls were out of stock, too.

No big deal. I've already got Jiffy cornbread mix in the pantry, and I can pick up rolls later if I feel like it.

A proper adventurer knows how to improvise.

🪙 The Real Trick

Here's the thing about these grocery deals: they're only bargains if you actually use the food.

I could spend twice as much assembling the perfect holiday spread, but would anyone enjoy it twice as much?

Probably not.

I'm not trying to win Thanksgiving. I'm trying to feed my family a good meal, enjoy the holiday, and not spend the following week regretting the grocery bill.

And that brings me to something I've been thinking about lately.

Groceries aren't getting cheaper. I'm just getting better at being poor.

It's funny, but there's some truth behind it.

When money gets tighter, you start paying closer attention. You notice promotions. You compare prices. You learn what you can substitute, what you can make yourself, and what isn't worth paying extra for.

Sometimes you find a genuinely good deal.

Sometimes you waste an hour chasing a bargain that saves you 37 cents.

And sometimes you manage to put together Thanksgiving dinner for about $25.

📜 Quest Status: Supplies Acquired

The turkey hasn't been cooked yet. The family hasn't gathered around the table. There's still a whole Thanksgiving dinner between this grocery haul and declaring victory.

But the supplies are secured, and the price was right.

For now, I'm calling that a successful shopping quest.

Total spent: Approximately $25

Advertised servings: 10

Cost per advertised serving: About $2.50

Not bad for a holiday feast.

Now let's hope I don't screw up the turkey.

Living well. Spending less. Gaining XP.
